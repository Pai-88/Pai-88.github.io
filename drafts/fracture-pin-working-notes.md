# Fracture pin page: working notes

Not part of the page. Three tools: the completeness sweep, the self-marking sheet, and the figure specs.

## 1. Completeness sweep

Every tissue the pin touches, against each phase. A cell with text is covered on the page; the location is in brackets. "Gap" means it is not yet on the page.

| Tissue | Insertion | Weeks in place | Removal | After |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skin | Punctured by the tip; entry site chosen away from nerves (3, Fig 6) | Held open: the infection track; rubbed by the protruding end; cap (3, row 8) | Pulled through the same hole, awake (5 row 9) | Small scar; infection resolves once the pin is out (4) |
| Subcutaneous fat | Depth sets the unsupported pin length (4, population variability) | Longer unsupported pin bends (4) | None significant | None significant |
| Muscle | Crossed by the pin (3) | Tethered if the pin pins a muscle belly (3) | Freed; stiffness recovers with movement (3) | Stiffness, mostly recovers (3) |
| Tendon | Must be avoided at entry (3) | Tethered tendon stops the joint (3) | Freed (3) | Scar to pin track possible (3) |
| Periosteum | Pierced; rich in pain fibres (2, 3) | Pressure on it is a pain source (3) | Pain on removal mostly from here (3) | Heals over the track; supplies callus (2) |
| Cortical bone | Grip comes from the two cortices; far-cortex plunge risk (2, Fig 6) | Holds the pin; too wide a wire splits a thin cortex (5 tension 1) | Smooth wire pulls free; no ingrowth (5 row 9) | Hole fills with bone in a few weeks (2 healing) |
| Cancellous bone | Little resistance, little grip (2) | Threaded pins grip here if needed (5 row 4) | None significant | Remodelling (2) |
| Osteocytes (bone cells) | Killed above 47 °C for 1 min: tip, speed, sharpness (2, row 3) | Dead ring loosens the pin and seeds infection (2) | None | Dead bone replaced by remodelling (2) |
| Marrow | None significant | Bone and marrow infection is the top grade of pin-track infection (3 skin) | None | None |
| Growth plate | Crossed routinely; threads and heat damage it (2, row 5) | Smooth wire only; no threads (row 5) | Smooth wire pulls free (row 5) | Growth arrest and bent arm if damaged; Salter–Harris (2) |
| Joint cartilage | Avoided; a pin in the joint grinds it (3) | Infection can enter the joint (3) | None | Cartilage does not repair (3) |
| Nerves | Ulnar at the medial entry, median and radial near the exit (3, Fig 5) | Pressure or tethering from a pin alongside (3) | None significant | Iatrogenic injury, 1 in 28 with a medial pin (4) |
| Brachial artery | In front of the fracture; plunge risk (3, Fig 5, Fig 6) | None significant | None | None |
| Immune system | Protein coating within minutes (3) | Macrophages and fibrous capsule; corrosion products inflame (3, row 6) | Capsule left behind | Resolves if no debris (3) |

Gaps found by the sweep and fixed on 14 Nov: marrow (added to the infection grading sentence), pin holes after removal (added to the healing paragraph). Remaining gap worth one sentence if the word count allows: the capsule and track left behind after removal.
